# HaoyuWu
Datasets processing


Two benchmark datasets were used in this study: (1) the MCI dataset, comprising participants with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and healthy controls (HCs), and (2) the MODMA dataset, comprising patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) and healthy controls. For both datasets, the preprocessed EEG recordings were segmented into 1-second epochs with a 50% overlap. A subject-wise data partitioning strategy was adopted, with 70% of the subjects assigned to the training set and the remaining 30% to the test set. Importantly, an explicit class-balancing strategy was applied to the training set to mitigate class imbalance and improve the robustness of model training.

MCI: Electrode locations were calibrated according to the standard 10–20 electrode montage. During preprocessing, each 30-minute EEG recording was filtered to suppress 50-Hz power-line interference and other signal artifacts. The resulting EEG signals were subsequently processed and segmented for downstream analysis.

MODMA: Electrode locations were calibrated using the GSN-HydroCel 129-channel electrode montage. Channels primarily associated with ocular activity (E1, E8, E14, E17, E21, E25, E32, E38, E43, E44, E48, E49, E56, E63, E64, E68, E69, E73, E74, E81, E82, E88, E89, E94, E95, E99, E107, E113, E114, E119, E120, E121, E125, E126, E127, and E128) were removed to reduce contamination from ocular artifacts. The EEG signals were then band-pass filtered between 0.1 and 30 Hz to attenuate low-frequency drifts and high-frequency noise while preserving the frequency range relevant to subsequent analyses.

To further reduce the influence of reference-related bias, the Cz channel was removed, and the EEG signals were re-referenced to the average of the remaining channels. Ocular and muscular artifacts were subsequently removed using automated blind source separation methods. Specifically, the second-order blind identification (SOBI) algorithm was employed for electrooculographic (EOG) artifact removal, whereas blind source separation based on canonical correlation analysis (BSS-CCA) was used to suppress electromyographic (EMG) artifacts.

Bad channels were automatically identified using statistical criteria, including kurtosis, and were reconstructed using spherical interpolation. In addition, independent component analysis (ICA) was applied to decompose the EEG signals into independent components. The resulting components were classified using ICLabel, and components associated with ocular and muscular activity were excluded to further minimize non-neural contamination. EEG segments exhibiting severe residual artifacts were also rejected. Finally, all cleaned EEG recordings were retained for subsequent analyses, providing high-quality signals for downstream processing and model development.
